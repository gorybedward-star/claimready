Lesson 1 of 7 · Benefits

How service connection works

What you'll learn: The three things VA looks for in most claims, and the different ways a condition can be connected to service.

Three key points

  1. Most claims need three things: a current disability, an event, injury, or disease in service, and a link between the two.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.303 Court decision Shedden v. Principi, 381 F.3d 1163 (Fed. Cir. 2004)

  2. For some conditions the law presumes the link, so no medical opinion on it is needed. Examples are certain chronic diseases that show up within a set time after discharge, and conditions tied to toxic exposures, including those added by the PACT Act.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.307; 38 C.F.R. § 3.309

  3. A condition can also be connected when a service-connected condition caused or worsened it (secondary), or when service made a condition you already had worse (aggravation).

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.310; 38 C.F.R. § 3.306

Lesson 2 of 7 · Benefits

Before you file

What you'll learn: How to protect your start date, and what evidence to gather before you file.

Three key points

  1. An intent to file gives you up to one year to file the full claim and can protect an earlier effective date.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.155(b)

  2. VA must help you get records. It requests federal records, such as service treatment records and VA treatment records. You can let VA request private records with VA Form 21-4142, or send them yourself.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 5103A; 38 C.F.R. § 3.159

  3. Your own statements, and statements from people who know you, are evidence of what you saw, did, and felt.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 1154(a) Court decision Jandreau v. Nicholson, 492 F.3d 1372 (Fed. Cir. 2007)

Lesson 3 of 7 · Benefits

Filing your claim

What you'll learn: How to fill out the claim form, and how to describe your conditions so nothing is missed.

Three key points

  1. A complete claim is filed on VA's required form. For disability compensation that is VA Form 21-526EZ.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.155; 38 C.F.R. § 3.160

  2. Describe each condition and its symptoms in plain words. VA reads a claim by what you describe, not only by the name you give it.

    Court decision Clemons v. Shinseki, 23 Vet. App. 1 (2009)

  3. You can file online, by mail, in person, or with free help from an accredited representative. No one may charge you for help preparing an initial claim.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 5904

Lesson 4 of 7 · Examinations

Your C&P exam

What you'll learn: When VA orders an exam, how to prepare, and what makes an exam or medical opinion adequate.

Three key points

  1. VA orders an exam when the record shows a current condition, something in service, and a sign they may be linked, but not enough to decide the claim.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 5103A(d); 38 C.F.R. § 3.159(c)(4) Court decision McLendon v. Nicholson, 20 Vet. App. 79 (2006)

  2. Go to the exam. If you miss it without good cause, VA may decide the claim on the record it has, or deny it.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.655

  3. A medical opinion needs a reasoned explanation, not just a conclusion. For joints, the exam should address painful motion and flare-ups.

    Court decision Nieves-Rodriguez v. Peake, 22 Vet. App. 295 (2008); Sharp v. Shulkin, 29 Vet. App. 26 (2017)

Lesson 5 of 7 · Ratings

How ratings work

What you'll learn: How VA matches your symptoms to rating criteria, and why ratings combine instead of adding up.

Three key points

  1. Each condition is rated under a diagnostic code. When your symptoms fall between two levels, VA assigns the higher one if your disability more nearly matches it.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 4.7

  2. Ratings combine instead of adding up. For example, 50% and 30% combine to 65%, which rounds to 70%, not 80%. The same symptoms can't be rated twice under different codes.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 4.25; 38 C.F.R. § 4.14

  3. When the evidence for and against is about equal, the veteran gets the benefit of the doubt.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)

Lesson 6 of 7 · Benefits

You got a decision

What you'll learn: How to read your decision, check that it covers everything, and pick a review option without losing your effective date.

Three key points

  1. VA's decision notice must tell you what was decided, the reasons, and your review options.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 5104; 38 C.F.R. § 3.103(f)

  2. There are three review options: a Supplemental Claim with new and relevant evidence (VA Form 20-0995), a Higher-Level Review of the same record (VA Form 20-0996), or an appeal to the Board (VA Form 10182).

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500; 38 C.F.R. § 3.2501; 38 C.F.R. § 3.2601; 38 C.F.R. § 20.202

  3. Filing a review option within one year of the decision generally keeps your original effective date. This is called continuous pursuit.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(h)

Lesson 7 of 7 · Benefits

Other benefits to know about

What you'll learn: The benefits beyond your rating percentage that are worth asking about.

Three key points

  1. If service-connected conditions keep you from holding a steady job, you may be paid at the 100% rate through Individual Unemployability (TDIU), claimed on VA Form 21-8940. The usual threshold is one condition at 60%, or 70% combined with one condition at 40%.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 4.16

  2. Special monthly compensation is added for things like loss of use, needing help with daily activities, or being housebound. VA Form 21-2680 is the exam form for aid and attendance or housebound status. A spouse who needs aid and attendance can add to the payment too.

    Law 38 U.S.C. § 1114(k)–(s); 38 U.S.C. § 1115; 38 C.F.R. § 3.350; 38 C.F.R. § 3.351; 38 C.F.R. § 3.352

  3. A temporary 100% rating can apply during a hospital stay of more than 21 days or recovery from certain surgery. Family benefits, such as Chapter 35 education and CHAMPVA, may follow a permanent and total rating.

    Law 38 C.F.R. § 4.29; 38 C.F.R. § 4.30
