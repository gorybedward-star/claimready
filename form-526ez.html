VA Form 21-526EZ, section by section

VA Form 21-526EZ is the application for disability compensation. This guide walks through what each section asks for and why it matters under public law, so you know what you are signing and what to have ready. It explains the form; it does not fill it out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Is this the right form for you?

Pick the one that fits. The answer comes from the form's own "When to Use this Form" instructions. Nothing you pick is saved.

Use VA Form 21-526EZ. This is what it is for. Read Protect your date first and the instructions on one page, then Section V, the heart of the form.

Use VA Form 21-526EZ for a claim for increase when the rating was decided more than one year ago. VA needs medical or lay evidence that the condition is worse and how the worsening affects your work. See the one-year look-back for increases.

Usually not this form. If you disagree with a decision from the past year, the form points you to a decision review: a Supplemental Claim (VA Form 20-0995) if you have new and relevant evidence, a Higher-Level Review (VA Form 20-0996), or a Board appeal (VA Form 10182). See ClaimReady's Supplemental Claims guide.

Use VA Form 20-0995, Supplemental Claim, not this form, when VA already decided the same or a similar benefit and you have new and relevant evidence. "New" means it was not in the record at the last decision; "relevant" means it tends to prove or disprove something at issue. See ClaimReady's Supplemental Claims guide.

Use VA Form 21-526EZ and look at the BDD program. Benefits Delivery at Discharge lets you file 90 to 180 days before you separate, with exams while you are still serving. See Item 1 for the BDD requirements.

Not this form. The form directs pension claims to VA Form 21P-527EZ and survivor claims (DIC, survivors pension, accrued benefits) to VA Form 21P-534EZ.

Before you start

Protect your date first

The date VA receives your claim usually controls how far back payments can go. Two rules can give you an earlier date, but only if you act in time.

Intent to file

An intent to file holds your date for one year. If VA receives a complete claim within that year, VA treats the claim as filed on the date it received the intent to file. You can submit one by starting the online application (a saved online application counts), by sending the intent-to-file form, or by telling VA orally. It does not need to name your conditions.

ClaimReady: Intent to file

The one-year window after service

If VA receives your claim within one year after you separate, payments for a condition granted on a direct basis can start the day after separation (or when entitlement arose). After that year, the date is generally the later of the date VA got the claim or the date entitlement arose.

Claims for increase

For a rated condition that got worse, VA can go back to the date the worsening is "factually ascertainable" from the evidence, but only if the claim or intent to file arrives within one year of that date.

If the form was incomplete

If VA receives an incomplete application, such as one without a signature, the date is protected only if you send a complete claim within one year.

Fictional example Dana separates in June 2025 and starts the online application in March 2026, which saves an intent to file. Dana finishes and submits the claim in August 2026. Because the claim arrived within a year of the intent to file, VA treats it as filed in March 2026, and because March 2026 is within a year of separation, a granted direct claim could reach back to the day after Dana's separation. This is a made-up illustration, not a VA decision.

Pages 1 to 8 on one page

The instructions most people skip

The first eight pages of the 21-526EZ are VA's notice of what evidence your claim needs, what you must do, and what VA will do. The law requires this notice, and because it comes with the form, VA does not send a separate first letter after you file. When you sign, you certify you received it. Here it is in one page.

1. Right form?

  • New claim, or an increase for a rating decided over a year ago: 21-526EZ.
  • Decided before and you have new and relevant evidence, or you disagree with a decision from the past year: 20-0995 Supplemental Claim, 20-0996 Higher-Level Review, or 10182 Board appeal.
  • Pension: 21P-527EZ. Survivors: 21P-534EZ.

2. How and where to send it

  • Online at VA.gov (parts can prefill; you can upload evidence and track status).
  • Mail: Department of Veterans Affairs, Evidence Intake Center, PO Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444.
  • An accredited VSO representative can help for free.

3. FDC: you send it all now

  • Send all private treatment records with the claim.
  • Name your VA and other federal treatment, and Guard or Reserve records.
  • Send anything later and VA moves the claim to the standard process. No risk to trying.

4. Standard: VA helps you get it

  • Sign 21-4142 and 21-4142a for private records you want VA to request.
  • You have one year from the date VA receives the claim to send evidence. If you send nothing in 30 days, VA may decide sooner, and you keep the rest of the year.

5. VA will

  • Get federal records you identify, such as VA medical center records.
  • Provide an exam or medical opinion if one is needed to decide the claim.
  • Standard process: make reasonable efforts for private and state records, and tell you if it can't get them.

6. Send these forms too, if they apply

  • Mental health: 21-0781. Unemployability: 21-8940 (and 21-4192). Vet Center treatment: 21-4142.
  • Dependents: 21-686c; child 18–23 in school: 21-674.
  • Aid and attendance: 21-2680, or 21-0779 for a nursing home. Housing: 26-4555. Auto: 21-4502.

7. What the evidence must show

8. When payments start

Generally when VA received the claim or when the evidence shows the level of disability. Claims received within one year of separation can start the day after separation, if the disability was present then.

9. How VA rates

0% to 100% under 38 C.F.R. Part 4, based on symptoms, how severe and long-lasting they are, and their effect on work. Helpful evidence: treatment records VA doesn't have, Social Security decisions, employer statements, and statements from people who see your symptoms.

You can ask for a hearing at any time. Questions: Ask VA online or 1-800-827-1000 (TTY: 711).

Item 1

Fully Developed Claim or standard process

Item 1 asks how you want VA to process the claim. It also offers two special programs for people still in the military.

FDC program (optional, faster)

  • You send all private treatment records with the claim.
  • You identify federal records, such as VA medical center records, and Guard or Reserve records.
  • VA gets the federal records and any exam or opinion it needs.
  • If you send more evidence later, or VA finds other records it needs, VA moves the claim to the standard process. The form says there is no risk to trying.

Standard process

  • You tell VA about evidence you want it to get, and sign VA Forms 21-4142 and 21-4142a for private records.
  • VA makes reasonable efforts to get private records: generally a first request and at least one follow-up.
  • VA keeps trying for federal records until they don't exist or more tries would be futile.
  • If VA can't get a record, it tells you. You are ultimately responsible for non-federal records.

Either way, VA's duty to assist applies. VA must provide an exam or medical opinion when one is necessary to decide the claim, for example when there is evidence of a current disability, an in-service event, and an indication that the two may be connected, but not enough medical evidence to decide.

IDES and BDD (still serving)
  • IDES: check this only if your military department referred you to the Integrated Disability Evaluation System.
  • BDD: file 90 to 180 days before separation; be available for exams for 45 days after filing; send the Separation Health Assessment Part A self-assessment, copies of your service treatment records for this period, and your expected release date. Filing close to the 180-day mark gives time for exams before you leave. With fewer than 90 days left, the claim goes through the regular process.

Before you send it

Six things a reviewer checks first

These are the first things a reviewer looks at on a 21-526EZ. Check them yourself before you send it. A problem here can delay the claim or cost you your date.

  1. Your identifying information is complete. Name, Social Security number, birth date, and service number in Section I, so VA can match the claim to you and your service records. Section I
  2. It is signed. No signature means it is not a complete claim. If you can't sign, use the witness or alternate signer sections. Section IX
  3. VA medical center records are listed. Name every VA medical center or military clinic where you were treated after discharge in Item 17, so VA knows which federal records to get. Item 17
  4. Your current mailing address is right. Item 10. Exam appointments and requests for evidence come by mail. Item 10
  5. The homeless box is checked if it applies. Item 14A or 14C. It tells VA you need a different way to be reached, and a point of contact. Section III
  6. Exposure is not a claim. Answering the toxic exposure questions in Section IV does not claim anything. Each condition must be listed in Item 16, for example "Asthma, burn pit exposure." Section IV

IYour identificationItems 2–12: name, SSN, prior claims, VA file number, birth date, service number, BDD release date, phone, address, email, VA employee

Why it matters

A complete claim must identify you and give VA enough to verify your service. The Privacy Act notice on page 14 says the Social Security number is required by law. Item 4 asks whether you ever filed with VA so VA can find your existing records.

Watch for

  • Item 8 is for BDD claims only: your date, or expected date, of release from active duty.
  • Item 11: checking the box lets VA send claim mail electronically. Use an email you check.
  • Item 12: if you work for VA, including work-study or internships, check the box.

Why it matters

Why it matters

Fill this in only if you are homeless or at risk of losing housing, for example housing that will be lost within 30 days. The point of contact in Items 14E and 14F gives VA a person who can reach you if mail can't.

Also worth knowing

IVToxic exposureItem 15: Gulf War and post-9/11 locations, herbicide locations, other exposures, dates

Why it matters

For many exposures, the law presumes certain diseases are connected to service, so you don't need a doctor's link. Answering Item 15 tells VA which presumptions and records to check. Page 4 of the notice lists the qualifying locations and dates, such as Vietnam and its offshore waters from January 9, 1962, to May 7, 1975, and Camp Lejeune for at least 30 days between August 1, 1953, and December 31, 1987.

Under the PACT Act, when you show a disability and a toxic exposure risk activity in service but the evidence doesn't yet establish service connection, VA must provide a medical exam and opinion that considers your total exposure across deployments.

Exposure is not a claim.Answering Section IV tells VA about your service; it does not claim a condition. List each condition you want decided in Item 16, and name the exposure there too, for example "Asthma, burn pit exposure."

Watch for

  • If you answer "Yes" in 15A, complete 15B through 15E.
  • Approximate dates, month and year, are acceptable.
  • 15D covers more than locations: asbestos, mustard gas, radiation, SHAD, job-related toxins (MOS), and Camp Lejeune water.
  • List every exposure period in 15E, not just the first.

Read more: ClaimReady's PACT Act summary and conditions VA has found no positive association with herbicides.

VYour claimed conditions and treatmentItems 16–17: the heart of the form

What Item 16 asks

For each condition, four things: the current disability or symptoms; the exposure, event, or injury it comes from; how it relates to service; and the approximate date it began or got worse. There are 15 lines, and Section XIII adds 20 more.

Why it matters

A complete claim must identify the benefit sought and describe the symptoms or medical conditions it is based on. What you list sets what VA looks at. VA also considers complications of a claimed condition and related "ancillary" benefits without you naming them, but naming each condition clearly is the safest way to make sure it is decided.

How to describe a condition

  • Symptoms are fine. The form asks for "disability(ies) or symptoms." You don't need a diagnosis to list something, for example "ringing in ears" or "low back pain."
  • Name secondary conditions. If one service-connected condition caused or worsened another, say so, as in the form's own example: "Left knee, secondary to right knee." Secondary service connection covers conditions caused by a service-connected disability and conditions it made worse beyond their natural progress.
  • Tie it to service. One line on what happened, such as "heavy equipment operator in service" for hearing loss.
  • Give a date, even if approximate. Month and year help VA know which records to look at.

What Item 17 asks

VA medical centers and military treatment facilities where you were treated after discharge for the conditions in Item 16, with an approximate start date. If treatment began in 2005 or later, you don't need dates in 17B.

Why it matters: these are federal records, and VA must keep trying to get them until they don't exist or more tries would be futile. If you leave a facility off, VA may not know to look. Private doctors go on VA Forms 21-4142 and 21-4142a, or send those records yourself.

VIService informationItems 18–23: other names, branch, component, dates, combat zone, Guard and Reserve, activation, POW

Why it matters

  • Dates decide the one-year effective date window, which presumptions apply, and which records VA requests. List every period of service in 20D.
  • Other names (18): records filed under a prior name can be missed.
  • Guard and Reserve (21–22): service connection for training periods follows different rules. For inactive duty training, it generally covers injuries, plus heart attack, cardiac arrest, or stroke, rather than any disease. Unit details help VA find records.
  • Former prisoners of war (23): certain diseases are presumed service connected.

Watch for

"Served in a combat zone" (Item 20C) is not the same thing as "engaged in combat with the enemy," which is the phrase the law uses for the special rule that lets a combat veteran's own account prove an in-service event. Background

Law 38 U.S.C. § 1154(b) VA form 21-526EZ pages 4 and 12

VIIRetired, separation, severance, and training payItems 24–28, including two "Do NOT pay me" boxes
Read Items 26 and 28 slowly.Checking Item 26 or Item 28 tells VA not to pay you VA compensation. If you already get compensation and check Item 26 while eligible for retired pay, the form says your compensation will be terminated. The form notes VA compensation is non-taxable and may be the greater benefit.

Why VA asks

  • Military retired pay (24–25): filing waives retired pay equal to the VA award, unless you qualify for concurrent receipt. By law, concurrent retirement pay generally applies to retirees with a service-connected rating of 50% or more, with special rules for disability (chapter 61) retirees.
  • Separation or disability severance pay (27): VA may withhold compensation to recoup it. For disability severance pay after January 28, 2008, there is no recoupment for disabilities incurred in a combat zone or in combat-related operations.
  • Training pay (28): you can't receive both drill pay and VA compensation for the same days. The form says keeping training pay and waiving VA benefits for those days is usually the better choice; if you check Item 28, VA adjusts the award for those days, which can create a debt.

Watch for

Report a lump sum you received, with the pre-tax amount. Leaving it off can create an overpayment later.

Law 10 U.S.C. § 1414(a); 38 C.F.R. § 3.700(a)(1), (a)(3), (a)(5) VA form 21-526EZ pages 12–13

VIIIDirect depositItems 29–32

Why it matters

The form says the Treasury requires federal benefits to be paid electronically. Skip this section if you already have direct deposit with VA. If you don't have a bank account, the form points to the Veterans Benefits Banking Program.

VA form 21-526EZ page 13

IX–XIISignaturesItems 33–37: your signature, witnesses, alternate signer, representative

Section IX: your signature (required)

A complete claim must be signed by you or someone legally authorized to sign for you. An unsigned form is not a complete claim. By signing, you certify the statements are true, authorize anyone with information about you to release it to VA, confirm you got the notice on pages 1–8, and confirm you sent your evidence, have none, or chose the standard process.

Section X: witnesses

Only if you sign with an "X." A signature by mark must be witnessed by two people who sign and give their addresses, or certified another way the regulation allows, such as by a notary.

Section XI: alternate signer

Only if Item 33A is blank. The law lets certain people sign for a claimant who is a minor, can't give accurate information because of mental incompetence, or is physically unable to sign: a court-appointed representative, a person responsible for the claimant's care such as a spouse, an agent under a durable power of attorney, or an institution's manager. VA Form 21-0972 must be on file or attached.

Section XII: representative

A representative can sign only if VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a is already on file, and the form says a representative can't sign an original claim. An accredited VSO representative helps for free. Agents and attorneys may not charge for work before you get notice of VA's initial decision.

Law 38 U.S.C. §§ 5101(a)(2), 5904(c)(1); 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.160(a)(2), 3.2130 VA form 21-526EZ pages 13–14

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Filing without an intent to file, then taking months to finish.
The date usually controls when payments can start. Starting the online form or filing an intent to file holds it for a year.
Using this form for a decision from the last year.
Disagreeing with a recent decision, or a previously denied claim with new evidence, generally goes through a decision review form instead.
Leaving off a secondary condition because "it isn't from service."
A condition caused or worsened by a service-connected condition can be service connected. Write "secondary to" and name the condition.
Waiting for a diagnosis before listing something.
The form asks for disabilities or symptoms. VA can get an exam if it needs one.
Answering the exposure questions but not listing a condition.
Exposure is not a claim. Each condition goes in Item 16.
Skipping Section IV.
Without exposure details, VA may not apply a presumption that fits your service.
Leaving VA clinics out of Item 17.
VA must look for federal records you identify; it can't look for ones it doesn't know about.
Checking Item 26 or 28 by mistake.
Both tell VA not to pay compensation.
Not signing, or having a representative sign an original claim.
An unsigned form is incomplete. You have a year to fix it, but your date is at risk.
Forgetting the extra forms.
Mental health claims, unemployability, dependents, and aid and attendance each have a form VA asks you to attach.
