VA Form 21-2680, aid and attendance or housebound exam, section by section

VA Form 21-2680 is a medical exam form. You fill out the first sections, and your own doctor or clinician fills out the exam. VA uses it to decide whether someone needs another person's regular help (aid and attendance) or is housebound, for special monthly compensation or special monthly pension. This guide explains each part and why it matters. It explains the form; it does not fill it out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Who is the exam for?

The form has boxes for self, spouse, parent, and child, and two benefits to choose from. Pick the one that fits. Nothing you pick is saved.

Check "Self" and "Special Monthly Compensation." The need for help must come from your service-connected conditions. Aid and attendance can support SMC at the (l) level; housebound can support SMC at the (s) level. See What VA decides.

Check "Self" and "Special Monthly Pension." For pension, the need for help can come from any condition, not only service-connected ones.

Check "Spouse" and "Special Monthly Compensation." Extra pay for a spouse who needs aid and attendance goes to a veteran rated 30% or more. Read Spouse aid and attendance.

Check "Self." A surviving spouse can get an added amount for aid and attendance with DIC, or for aid and attendance or housebound status with survivors pension. Choose the benefit that matches what you receive.

Before you start

What VA decides from this exam

Aid and attendance

Needing another person's regular help with daily life. Any one of these can show it:

  • Corrected vision of 5/200 or less in both eyes, or a visual field of 5 degrees or less.
  • Being a patient in a nursing home because of mental or physical incapacity.
  • A factual need: unable to dress, bathe, feed oneself, use the toilet, or adjust a prosthetic without help; needing protection from everyday hazards; or bedridden because of the condition.

Not every factor has to be present, and the need must be regular, not constant.

Housebound

Either a single disability rated 100% plus other disabilities independently rated 60% or more, or permanently housebound: substantially confined to home and its immediate premises because of disability, and reasonably certain to stay that way.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.351(b)–(d), 3.352(a), 3.350(b)(3), 3.350(i)

The form on one page

What the 21-2680 asks

1. You (Sections I–V)

  • Veteran's and claimant's identification, and relationship.
  • Who it's for (self, spouse, parent, child) and one benefit: SMC or SMP.
  • Whether the claimant is in a hospital now, with dates and place.
  • Sign and date Item 15 (the date is required).

2. Who can examine

  • A medical doctor (MD), doctor of osteopathic medicine (DO), physician assistant, or advanced practice registered nurse (APRN).
  • Usually your own provider.

3. Diagnoses

  • Each diagnosis with its most significant symptoms (Item 17). They must match the level of help described later.
  • Which conditions are permanent and totally disabling (Item 18).
  • Which conditions restrict each activity, with the reason (Item 25).

4. Daily help

  • Hours in bed, if confined (Item 26).
  • Help needed, night (9 PM–9 AM) and day (9 AM–9 PM): bathing, toileting, eating, transfers, medications, hygiene, walking in the home, dressing, other (Item 27).

5. Body and safety

  • Legal blindness and corrected vision, each eye (Item 28).
  • Nursing home care needed (29); ability to manage payments (30).
  • Arms, legs, spine; bowel or bladder, dizziness, memory, balance (31–35).

6. Leaving home and signing

  • How often and under what conditions the claimant leaves home (36); canes, braces, or a person needed, and how far (37).
  • Examiner's printed name, title, signature (required), date, NPI, and facility (38–45).

Before you send it

What a reviewer checks first

Gaps here can mean VA orders its own exam or asks your provider to redo the form.

1. Both parts are signed.

You sign and date Item 15. The examiner gives a printed name, title, signature, date, and NPI number in Items 38–42. The examiner must be an MD, DO, PA, or APRN. Section V · Sections VII–VIII

2. The diagnoses explain the help described.

The form says the diagnoses in Item 17 must match the level of help in Items 26–37, and Item 25 asks which condition restricts each activity and why. A checked box with no diagnosis behind it is weak evidence. Section VI

Also check

  • One benefit is chosen in Item 13, SMC or SMP, and the right claimant box is checked. Section II
  • Both time periods are covered in Item 27, night and day.
  • Corrected vision is given for each eye if vision is the issue (Item 28B).
  • Leaving home is described (Item 36) if housebound status is the issue.

VA form 21-2680 Section I

II–IIIWho it's for and which benefitItem 13: choose one

What it asks

Whether the exam is for the veteran (self), a spouse, a parent, or a child, and one benefit: Special Monthly Compensation (SMC) or Special Monthly Pension (SMP).

Why it matters

SMC is paid with service-connected compensation or DIC; for a veteran's own SMC, the need for help must come from service-connected conditions. SMP is an added amount for people eligible for pension, and any condition can count.

VA form 21-2680 Section V

VIExaminationItems 16–37: the heart of the form

Who completes it

An MD, DO, physician assistant, or APRN. The form asks for detailed findings: whether the claimant is blind or bedridden, how they walk, where they go, and what they do on a typical day.

What makes the exam useful

  • Diagnoses tied to function. Each diagnosis with its most significant symptoms (17), which ones are permanent and totally disabling (18), and which condition restricts each activity and why (25).
  • Specific help, day and night. Item 27 lists bathing, toileting, eating, transfers, medications, hygiene, walking in the home, dressing, and other tasks, separately for 9 PM–9 AM and 9 AM–9 PM.
  • Safety. Memory loss, dizziness, poor balance, and loss of bowel or bladder control (35) bear on whether the claimant needs protection from everyday hazards.
  • Vision with correction for each eye (28B), since the test is corrected vision.
  • Leaving home. How often and under what conditions (36), and whether a cane, brace, or person is needed and for how far (37). This bears on housebound status.
  • Managing money. Item 30 asks whether the claimant can manage benefit payments. See ClaimReady's competency article for what VA does with that.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.351(c), 3.352(a), 3.350(i)(2) VA form 21-2680 Section VI and examiner note

VII–VIIIExaminer's signature and informationItems 38–45

What it asks

The examiner's printed name, title, signature (required), date, NPI number, and the medical facility's name, address, and phone. A penalty notice applies to knowingly false statements.

Why it matters

VA needs to confirm the examiner is one of the qualified provider types and be able to follow up if something is unclear.

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Boxes checked with no diagnosis behind them.
The form asks the examiner to tie each restriction to a condition and explain why.
Only daytime help described.
Item 27 asks about nights too. Night needs, such as toileting or wandering, often matter.
Vision given without correction.
The test uses corrected vision in both eyes.
Thinking the need has to be around the clock.
The need must be regular, not constant, and not every factor has to apply.
Missing examiner signature, title, or NPI.
VA may not be able to use the form without them.
Choosing both benefits, or the wrong one.
Item 13 takes one: SMC or SMP.
