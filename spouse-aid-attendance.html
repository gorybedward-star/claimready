When your spouse needs daily help: extra VA compensation for you

If you get VA disability compensation and your spouse needs another person's help with daily life, is nearly blind, or lives in a nursing home, VA can pay you an extra monthly amount. This is often called special monthly spouse's compensation, or spousal aid and attendance (A&A). It is easy to miss because it depends on your spouse's health, not yours. This article explains the two things that must be true, what evidence helps, and when the extra pay can start.

Based on public sources: 38 U.S.C. § 1115, 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.4(b)(2), 3.351, 3.352(a), and 3.401(a)(3), VA's published rate tables and forms, and VA's procedures manual, M21-1, Part VIII, Subpart iv, Chapter 4, Section A, Topic 12. Start here

Two things must be true

The extra pay goes to you, the veteran, "by reason of the veteran's spouse being in need of aid and attendance." Your spouse's condition does not have to be related to your service, or to any service.

  1. You are rated 30% or more.Your combined service-connected rating must be at least 30%. You do not need to be rated 100%. Below 30%, VA does not pay extra for any dependents, including this.
  2. Your spouse meets one of three tests.Nearly blind, a patient in a nursing home because of mental or physical incapacity, or a factual need for another person's regular help. See the three tests.

Your spouse also needs to be on your award as a dependent. If you are already paid at the "with spouse" rate, that part is done.

Law 38 U.S.C. § 1115; 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.4(b)(2), 3.351(a)(2) VA guidance M21-1 VIII.iv.4.A.12.a–b

The 30% line comes first. Below a combined 30% rating, VA does not pay extra for a spouse, even one who needs aid and attendance. If your rating later goes up to 30% or more, this benefit becomes possible.

Requirement 1

The 30% requirement

The law allows extra compensation for a spouse, children, or dependent parents only when the veteran is entitled to compensation for disability "evaluated as 30 per centum or more disabling." VA's manual applies the same line to the spouse aid and attendance amount.

What counts

  • Your combined rating for all service-connected conditions, not any single condition.
  • Any rating from 30% to 100%. The added amount is larger at higher ratings.

What does not change it

  • Whether your spouse's condition has anything to do with military service.
  • Whether you already get special monthly compensation for yourself.

Law 38 U.S.C. § 1115; 38 C.F.R. § 3.4(b)(2) VA guidance M21-1 VIII.iv.4.A.12.b

Requirement 2

The three tests for your spouse

Your spouse needs to meet any one of these. They are the same tests VA uses for a veteran's own aid and attendance.

  • Vision: blind or nearly blindCorrected visual acuity of 5/200 or less in both eyes, or concentric contraction of the visual field to 5 degrees or less. In plain terms, 5/200 means seeing at 5 feet what a person with normal vision sees at 200 feet, even with glasses. A 5-degree field is a very narrow tunnel of sight.
  • Nursing home: a patient because of incapacityA patient in a nursing home because of mental or physical incapacity. For VA, a nursing home means a state-licensed facility giving skilled or intermediate nursing care, a nursing home unit in an approved State Veterans Home, or a VA nursing home unit. Assisted living or a group home may not fit this definition; if so, the factual need test can still apply.
  • Factual need: regular help from another personDisabilities that create an actual need for another person's regular aid and attendance, judged by the factors below.
The factual need factors (38 C.F.R. § 3.352(a))
  • Cannot dress or undress, or keep ordinarily clean and presentable.
  • Often needs help adjusting a prosthetic or orthopedic device that they cannot adjust alone. (Ordinary items like supports, belts, or lacing at the back do not count.)
  • Cannot feed themselves because of lost coordination of the arms or hands, or extreme weakness.
  • Cannot attend to the "wants of nature" (toileting) without help.
  • Needs regular care or help to stay safe from the hazards or dangers of daily life because of a physical or mental condition, such as dementia.
  • Bedridden, meaning the condition itself requires staying in bed. Choosing to rest in bed, or bed rest a doctor prescribes to help recovery, does not count.

Not every factor has to be present. VA looks at the functions your spouse cannot do along with their whole condition. The need must be regular; it does not have to be constant, around the clock.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.351(b)–(c), 3.352(a), 3.1(z) (nursing home) VA guidance M21-1 VIII.iv.4.A.12.a

Examinations and evidence

What evidence helps

VA decides based on medical evidence about your spouse. Your spouse is not a veteran, so the evidence usually comes from their own doctors.

  1. Make sure your spouse is on your award. If not, add them as a dependent (VA uses VA Form 21-686c for this).
  2. Ask your spouse's provider to complete VA Form 21-2680. It is VA's form for an aid and attendance or housebound exam, and it has a box for a spouse claimant. You fill out the first sections; a medical doctor, doctor of osteopathy, physician assistant, or advanced practice registered nurse fills out the exam sections.
  3. If your spouse is in a nursing home, send proof that they are a patient there and why, such as a statement from the facility. VA can grant this once it confirms the nursing home status.
  4. VA may ask for more. If the evidence is not enough to decide, VA can request an examination of your spouse.

What the doctor should include

  • Each daily task your spouse needs help with, how often, and why (the diagnosis behind it).
  • Safety: whether your spouse can be left alone, and what hazards they cannot handle, such as the stove, medications, or wandering.
  • For vision: corrected visual acuity in each eye, and visual field results if the field is narrowed.
  • For a nursing home: that your spouse is a patient there because of mental or physical incapacity.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.351(c), 3.352(a) VA forms VA Form 21-2680 (Jun 2026 edition); VA Form 21-686c VA guidance M21-1 VIII.iv.4.A.12.a (note and references)

Effective date

When the extra pay can start

The general rule is the later of two dates: the date VA receives the claim, or the date your spouse's entitlement arose (when the need for aid and attendance began).

There is one helpful exception. If VA grants your own disability compensation back to a date before your claim was received, the spouse amount can also be paid for any part of that earlier period in which your spouse's need for aid and attendance is shown.

Fictional example Dana is rated 50%. Dana's spouse moved into a state-licensed nursing home in March because of advanced Parkinson's disease. Dana files in August with a statement from the facility. Under the general rule, the earliest the extra pay can start is the August date VA received the claim, because that is later than March. Waiting does not change what qualifies, but it can cost months of payments. This is a made-up illustration, not a VA decision.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.401(a)(3) VA guidance M21-1 VIII.iv.4.A.12.a (references)

Amount

How it is paid

VA's rate tables list "Spouse receiving Aid and Attendance" as an added amount on top of your basic monthly rate with a spouse. It grows with your combined rating. For rates effective December 1, 2025, the added amount ranged from about $61 a month at 30% to about $201 a month at 100%. Rates change each year, so check VA's current compensation rates.

Law 38 U.S.C. § 1115(1)(E), (2) VA guidance VA compensation rate tables (effective Dec 1, 2025)

Common misunderstandings

What people often get wrong

"I have to be rated 100%."
No. Any combined rating of 30% or more can qualify.
"My spouse's condition has to be from my service."
No. The test is your spouse's need for help, whatever the cause.
"My spouse has to need care every hour of the day."
No. The need must be regular, not constant, and not every factor has to apply.
"This is the same as my own SMC."
No. Your own special monthly compensation is based on your service-connected disabilities. This amount is based on your spouse and is paid in addition.
"VA will add it automatically when my spouse gets sick."
No. VA needs a claim and evidence. Because pay usually starts no earlier than the claim date, it helps to apply once your spouse meets a test.
"This is for surviving spouses too."
Not this benefit. A surviving spouse who needs aid and attendance may get it with DIC or Survivors Pension, under separate rules. See ClaimReady's Aid and attendance topic.
