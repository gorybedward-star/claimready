VA Form 20-0996, Higher-Level Review, section by section

VA Form 20-0996 asks a more experienced VA reviewer to take a fresh look at a decision using the same evidence. You can't add new evidence, but you can point out errors, including in an optional informal conference. This guide explains what each section asks for and why it matters under public law. It explains the form; it does not fill it out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Is this the right form for you?

Pick the one that fits. Nothing you pick is saved.

Use VA Form 20-0996. A Higher-Level Review is a new look at the same record by a reviewer who wasn't part of the first decision. List each issue and decision date, and consider an informal conference to point out the error.

Use a Supplemental Claim instead. The higher-level reviewer can't consider evidence received after the decision notice. See ClaimReady's VA Form 20-0995 guide.

Too late for this form. VA must receive a Higher-Level Review within one year of the decision notice. A Supplemental Claim with new and relevant evidence has no deadline, though the effective date then generally starts no earlier than when VA receives it.

Use VA Form 10182 for a Board appeal. See ClaimReady's VA Form 10182 guide.

Before you start

How a Higher-Level Review works

What the reviewer does

  • An experienced adjudicator who took no part in the prior decision reviews it fresh, giving it no deference. Favorable findings already made still stand.
  • Usually at a different office. You can ask for the same office, and VA will agree unless it has good cause not to.
  • Can grant based on a difference of opinion, but won't make the outcome worse on that basis alone.

What the reviewer can't do

  • Consider evidence received after the decision notice.
  • Gather new evidence, except to fix a duty-to-assist error. If VA missed a step it owed you, such as getting a record or an adequate exam, and can't grant the full benefit, the reviewer sends the claim back to correct the error and decide again.
  • One year. VA must receive the form within one year of the decision notice. Filing on time keeps the issue continuously pursued and protects your effective date.
  • One lane at a time. You can't have a Supplemental Claim and a Higher-Level Review pending on the same issue.
  • Incomplete form? VA tells you what's missing. A complete form within 60 days of that notice, or before the year ends, keeps the original date.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.2500(b), (h)(1); 3.2601(d)–(g), (i)–(j); 3.104(c)

The instructions on one page

What the 20-0996 instructions say

1. When to use it

  • To ask for a Higher-Level Review of a decision you received.
  • One benefit type per form.

2. Deadline

  • VA must receive it within one year of the date of the decision notice.
  • Responding to an older Statement of the Case: within 60 days, which withdraws those issues from the legacy system.

3. Where to send it

  • To the office or processing center named on your decision letter, or online at VA.gov.
  • Keep a copy of everything you send.

4. Informal conference

  • Optional. One per review. To point out errors of fact or law, not to add evidence.
  • Check 16A and a morning or afternoon preference in 16B. VA makes two attempts to call.
  • Want VA to call your representative? Give their name and phone in Item 17.
  • A written statement of the errors is an alternative that can avoid delay.

5. Issues

  • List each issue (18A) and the decision letter date (18B). Both are required.
  • Only listed issues are reviewed.
  • No evidence received after the decision notice is considered.

6. Signatures

  • Sign Item 19A (not in pencil).
  • Alternate signer: attach VA Form 21-0972.
  • Representative: VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a must be on file or attached.

Before you send it

What a reviewer checks first

The form marks these as required. A problem with either one stops or delays the review.

1. It is signed and dated (Item 19).

A request must be signed by you or someone authorized to sign for you to be complete. Section VI

2. Every issue is listed with its decision letter date (Items 18A and 18B).

The reviewer looks only at the issues you list, and the date shows the request is within one year. Section V

Also check

  • One benefit type is checked in Item 15. Section III
  • It reaches VA within one year of the decision notice.
  • If you asked for an informal conference, a contact preference is checked and your representative's phone is listed if VA should call them. Section IV
  • No new evidence is attached. It won't be considered here; use a Supplemental Claim for that.

I–IIVeteran and claimant informationItems 1–14

What it asks

Section I is the veteran's information. Section II is only for a claimant who is not the veteran, such as a surviving spouse. There is also a box to tell VA if you are homeless.

Why it matters

A complete request must identify the claimant and their relationship to the veteran. A current phone number matters if you ask for an informal conference: VA makes two attempts to reach you.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2601(c) VA form 20-0996 Sections I–II

IIIBenefit typeItem 15: select only one

Why it matters

Each form covers one benefit type, such as compensation or pension. Use a separate form for each benefit type you want reviewed.

VA form 20-0996 Item 15

IVOptional informal conferenceItems 16–17

What it is

A phone call in which you or your representative explain to the reviewer the errors of fact or law you see in the decision. VA holds only one per request and won't accept new evidence or new facts during it.

What it asks

  • 16A: check the box if you want one.
  • 16B: pick one contact preference, mornings or afternoons in your time zone, for you or your representative. VA makes two attempts to reach the person chosen.
  • 17: if VA should call your representative, give their name, phone, and email.

Worth knowing

The instructions say a written statement explaining the errors is an alternative that can avoid delays from scheduling a call.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2601(h) VA form 20-0996 Section IV

VIssues for reviewItem 18: the heart of the form

What it asks

Each specific issue you disagree with (18A) and the date of the VA decision notification letter (18B). The form marks both required.

Why it matters

Only the issues you list are reviewed. Copy each issue the way the decision letter states it, such as "Evaluation of left knee condition" or "Service connection for tinnitus." The date shows the request is within one year.

Watch for

  • If you're responding to a legacy Statement of the Case, you have 60 days, and filing this form withdraws those issues from the legacy appeal.
  • Describe the error, if you can, in a short statement. It helps the reviewer see what you think went wrong.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2601(c), (i) VA form 20-0996 Section V

VI–VIISignaturesItems 19–20

Section VI: your signature

Sign and date Item 19. The instructions ask you not to sign in pencil. A request must be signed by the claimant or a person authorized to sign for them.

Section VII: representative

A representative can sign only if VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a is already on file or attached. If someone else signs for a claimant who can't, include VA Form 21-0972, Alternate Signer Certification.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2601(c) VA form 20-0996 Sections VI–VII

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Not signing, or leaving out an issue's decision date.
Both are required for a complete request.
Attaching new evidence.
The reviewer can't consider it. If the new evidence is the point, file a Supplemental Claim instead.
Bringing new facts to the informal conference.
The conference is for pointing out errors in the decision as it stands.
Missing the one-year deadline.
After a year, a Higher-Level Review isn't available for that decision.
Filing a Supplemental Claim on the same issue at the same time.
Only one review of an issue can be pending at once.
Worrying the review will lower your rating.
The reviewer won't change the outcome to your disadvantage based only on a difference of opinion, and favorable findings stand. A clear and unmistakable error is handled under a separate rule.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.2500(b), 3.2601(c), (f), (h), (j); 3.104(c); 3.105(a)
