VA Forms 21-4142 and 21-4142a, private records release, section by section

These two forms work together. 21-4142 is your permission for a private doctor, hospital, or other source to send records to VA. 21-4142a tells VA who those providers are and where to find them. This guide explains each section and why it matters under public law. It explains the forms; it does not fill them out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Before you start

What VA does with your permission

VA's part

  • Makes reasonable efforts to get the private records you identify and authorize: generally a first request and at least one follow-up.
  • Doesn't pay a provider's fee for records.
  • Tells you if it can't get a record, what it tried, and what you can do.

Your part

  • Give enough information to find the records: provider name, address, conditions, and approximate dates.
  • Sign the authorization.
  • You are ultimately responsible for getting private records to VA, so sending them yourself is often faster.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.159(c), (c)(1), (e)

The forms on one page

What the 21-4142 and 21-4142a say

1. When to use them

  • To let VA request private records for your claim.
  • Not needed for VA medical records, or if you already have the records or will get them yourself.

2. 21-4142: permission

  • Section I: veteran. Section II: the patient, if not the veteran.
  • Section III: sources it covers, such as doctors, social workers, employers, and insurers.
  • Section IV: what may be released.

3. What may be released

  • All medical records, including mental health (not psychotherapy notes), substance use, HIV/AIDS, sickle cell, and genetic tests, and how the condition affects daily tasks and work.
  • Past records, plus records created in the 12 months after signing.

4. Limits, time, revoking

  • Item 12: write any limit. Blank means no limit.
  • Expires 12 months after signing.
  • Revoke in writing to VA and to each source. Records already sent can still be used.

5. 21-4142a: providers

  • For each provider: name, conditions treated, dates, and full address.
  • Room for several providers on one form.

6. Sign and send

  • Items 13 and 14, the signature and date, are required. Someone signing for you completes Item 16.
  • Compensation: Evidence Intake Center, PO Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444, or upload online.

Before you send it

What a reviewer checks first

A gap here means VA can't send the request, or the provider can't find the records.

1. The 21-4142 is signed and dated (Items 13–14).

Both items are marked required. Without them, the provider has no permission to release anything. Section V

2. Each provider is fully listed on the 21-4142a.

Name, conditions treated, dates, and a complete street address for every provider. An incomplete address means the request can't reach them. 21-4142a Section III

Also check

  • Item 12 doesn't block what VA needs. A limit can keep out the very records that support the claim.
  • The authorization hasn't expired. It lasts 12 months. If a claim runs longer and VA still needs records, a new one may be needed.
  • VA records aren't listed here. They don't need this form; list them on the claim form instead.

What it asks

The veteran's identification. Section II is only if the records are about someone else, such as a spouse or child, for example for spouse aid and attendance or a helpless child.

VA form 21-4142 Sections I–II

4142 III–IVSources and records covered

Sources (Section III)

Medical sources, social workers, examiners VA uses, employers, insurers, and others who know about the condition.

Records (Section IV)

All medical records, including information on mental health (except psychotherapy notes), substance use, sickle cell anemia, communicable diseases including HIV/AIDS, and genetic testing, plus how the condition affects daily tasks and work. It covers past records and records created within 12 months after you sign.

Privacy

Once a source sends records to VA, they are no longer covered by the HIPAA Privacy Rule but are protected by the federal Privacy Act. A provider can't require you to sign this authorization before treating you.

VA form 21-4142 Sections III–IV and authorization text

4142 VAuthorization, limits, and signatureItems 12–16

Item 12: limits

Write any limit on what can be released. If blank, there is no limit. Think twice before limiting: a limit can keep out records that support your claim.

Items 13–16: signing

The signature and date are required. If someone else signs, Item 16 asks for their relationship and full name, title, organization, and address; a court appointment needs the docket number, county, and state.

How long it lasts

12 months from the date signed, unless you revoke it. To revoke, write to the VA office handling your claim (or the Board, if on appeal) and send a copy to each source. VA can still use records released before you revoked.

VA form 21-4142 Section V

4142aMedical provider informationItems 1–13

What it asks

Your identification (and the patient's, if different), then for each provider: name, conditions you were treated for, dates of treatment, and full address.

Why it matters

This is the address list VA uses to send requests. VA must make reasonable efforts to get records you adequately identify, so a complete name and address is what turns your permission into an actual request.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.159(c)(1) VA form 21-4142a Sections I–III

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Using the 21-4142 for VA records.
VA gets its own records. List VA facilities on your claim form instead.
Using it when you already have the records.
The form warns this slows processing. Send them.
Not signing or dating the 21-4142.
Both are required.
Incomplete provider addresses on the 21-4142a.
VA can't request records it can't route.
A limit in Item 12 that blocks key records.
Leave it blank unless you have a specific reason.
Assuming VA will keep trying forever.
For private records, VA generally makes a first request and at least one follow-up, then tells you if it couldn't get them.
