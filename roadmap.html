Your claim roadmap: the basics

A VA disability claim moves through seven stages. Find yours to see the one thing that matters most there and where to start. Each point shows the public law or VA guidance behind it, or says when it is a general tip. For the rules explained in more depth, see Your claim, step by step.

1

Leaving the military

You are here if: You have 180 days or less left in service, or you separated within the last year.

Most important: A claim received within one year after separation can take an effective date of the day after you left service.Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.400(b)(2)(i)

Start here: Intent to file →

2

Getting ready to file

You are here if: You have a condition you believe is related to service, and you have not filed for it yet.

Most important: Send an intent to file. It gives you up to one year to file the full claim and can protect an earlier effective date.Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.155(b)

Start here: Claim checklist →

3

Filing your claim

You are here if: You are ready to send your claim, or you are filling out the form now.

Most important: File on VA Form 21-526EZ, complete and signed. An unsigned form is not a complete claim.Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.155; 38 C.F.R. § 3.160 VA guidance VA Form 21-526EZ instructions

Start here: Form 21-526EZ guide →

4

Your C&P exam

You are here if: VA scheduled a compensation and pension (C&P) exam, or you expect one.

Most important: Go to the exam, or contact VA before it to reschedule. Missing it without good cause can lead VA to decide on the record it has, or to deny the claim.Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.655

Start here: C&P exams: the complete guide →

5

Your decision arrives

You are here if: VA sent you a decision letter or rating decision.

Most important: Note the decision date. Most review options keep your effective date only if filed within one year of it.Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(h)

Start here: Decode your decision letter →

6

If you disagree

You are here if: You disagree with all or part of a decision, or think a rating is too low.

Most important: File within one year of the decision to keep your original effective date.Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(h)

Start here: Lesson 6: You got a decision →

7

After your rating

You are here if: You have one or more service-connected conditions and want to know what else to consider.

Most important: Ask about benefits beyond the percentage: Individual Unemployability, special monthly compensation, a temporary 100% rating, and family benefits.Law 38 C.F.R. § 4.16; 38 C.F.R. § 3.350; 38 C.F.R. § 4.29; 38 C.F.R. § 4.30

Start here: Lesson 7: Other benefits →

Many veterans are in two stages at once, such as rated for one condition and filing for another. Use the stage that fits each condition.
