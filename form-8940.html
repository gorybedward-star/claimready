VA Form 21-8940, Individual Unemployability, section by section

VA Form 21-8940 is the application for total disability based on individual unemployability (TDIU or IU): compensation at the 100% rate when service-connected conditions keep you from holding a substantially gainful job, even if your combined rating is lower. This guide explains what each section asks for and why it matters under public law. It explains the form; it does not fill it out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Before you start

What VA looks at

The 21-8940 gives VA your work and medical history. VA uses it to decide two things.

  1. The percentage rulesGenerally one service-connected condition at 60% or more, or two or more with one at 40% or more and a combined 70% or more. Some conditions count as one: both arms or both legs, a common cause or single accident, one body system, injuries in action, or as a prisoner of war.
  2. Can't work because of service-connected conditionsYour service-connected conditions alone must keep you from getting or keeping a substantially gainful job. VA does not count age or conditions that aren't service connected.

Marginal work doesn't count against you

Marginal employment is not substantially gainful. It generally exists when your yearly earned income is at or below the Census Bureau's poverty threshold for one person. It can also be found above that line on the facts, such as a protected job in a family business or a sheltered workshop.

Below the percentages?

If you are unemployable because of service-connected conditions but don't meet the percentages, the rule says the case should go to VA's Director of Compensation Service for extra-schedular consideration.

The form on one page

What the 21-8940 asks and says

1. What it is

  • A claim for total disability because of service-connected conditions.
  • "Answer all questions fully and accurately."
  • Often filed with a 21-526EZ, which lists 21-8940 and 21-4192 for unemployability.

2. Disability and treatment

  • Item 8: which service-connected conditions keep you from working.
  • Items 9–13: doctors and hospitals in the past 12 months, with dates.

3. Work history

  • When your disability affected full-time work, when you last worked full time, and when you became too disabled to work.
  • Item 18: every job, including self-employment, for the last five years you worked, with hours, dates, time lost to illness, and earnings.

4. Income and benefits

  • Earned income for the past 12 months, and current monthly income if working.
  • Whether you left your last job because of disability (explain in Remarks), and any disability retirement or workers' comp.
  • Jobs you applied for since becoming too disabled.

5. Education

  • Highest grade completed.
  • Training before and since you became too disabled to work.

6. Signature and duties

  • Sign Item 27 (required). Two witnesses if you sign with an "X."
  • You certify you can't secure or follow a substantially gainful occupation.
  • If granted, you must tell VA right away if you return to work, or payments after that may become a debt.
  • Mail: Evidence Intake Center, PO Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444.

IIDisability and medical treatmentItems 8–13

What it asks

Item 8: the service-connected conditions that keep you from working. Items 9–13: whether you've seen a doctor or been hospitalized in the past 12 months, with names, addresses, and dates. More dates go in Item 26.

Why it matters

Only service-connected conditions count. Naming them, and the treatment that shows how they limit you, tells VA what to look at. If a condition isn't service connected yet, it needs its own claim.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 4.16(a) VA form 21-8940 Section II

IIIEmployment statementItems 14–22: the heart of the form

The dates (Items 14–16)

When your disability began affecting full-time work, when you last worked full time, and when you became too disabled to work. These can bear on when entitlement arose.

Earnings and history (Items 17–18)

The most you ever earned in a year, and every job, including self-employment and military duty, for the last five years you worked: employer, type of work, hours, dates, time lost to illness, and highest monthly earnings. Use Remarks if you need more room.

Now (Items 19–22)

  • Guard or Reserve: whether your condition keeps you from military duties.
  • Earned income for the past 12 months, and current monthly earnings if working. Work at or below the one-person poverty threshold is generally marginal, not substantially gainful.
  • Whether you left your last job because of your disability (explain in Item 26), and whether you get or expect disability retirement or workers' compensation.
  • Jobs you've applied for since becoming too disabled to work.

Watch for

VA may ask your last employers for information; the 21-526EZ lists VA Form 21-4192, the employer's form, alongside the 21-8940. Give complete employer addresses so the request reaches them.

VI–VIIAuthorization, certification, signature, where to sendItems 27–30

What you agree to

  • You authorize release of information to VA.
  • You certify you are unable to secure or follow any substantially gainful occupation, and that your statements are true and complete.
  • If granted, you must tell VA immediately if you return to work. Payments after you start work may be treated as an overpayment you have to repay.

Signing

Sign Item 27 (required) and date Item 28. If you sign with an "X," two witnesses who know you must sign in ink and give their addresses.

Where to send

Department of Veterans Affairs, Evidence Intake Center, PO Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444, or online at VA.gov.

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Leaving the employment history blank or partial.
VA needs it to contact employers and judge past work. Use Remarks for more room.
Listing conditions that aren't service connected in Item 8.
Only service-connected conditions count toward unemployability.
Thinking any work at all rules you out.
Marginal employment is not substantially gainful, and the question is whether you can get and keep substantially gainful work.
Thinking you must meet the percentages exactly.
If service-connected conditions keep you from working but the percentages aren't met, the rule calls for extra-schedular consideration.
Not telling VA about a return to work.
The form requires it. Late notice can create a debt.
Not signing.
The signature is required.
