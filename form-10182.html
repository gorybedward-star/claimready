VA Form 10182, Board Appeal, section by section

VA Form 10182, the Notice of Disagreement, asks the Board of Veterans' Appeals to have a Veterans Law Judge decide your appeal. On it you pick one of three dockets: direct review, evidence submission, or a hearing. That choice decides what evidence the judge sees and how long you may wait. This guide explains what each part asks for and why it matters under public law. It explains the form; it does not fill it out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Is this the right form for you?

Pick the one that fits. Nothing you pick is saved.

Use VA Form 10182. List each issue and its decision date, then check one docket in Part II. See the three dockets to choose.

Compare two paths. A Supplemental Claim (VA Form 20-0995) sends the new evidence back to VA, which is often quicker. A Board appeal on the evidence submission or hearing docket puts it before a judge. Both protect your effective date if filed within one year.

Probably too late for this form. The Board must receive it within one year of the decision letter. You can ask the Board in writing for an extension for good cause, and the Board decides. A Supplemental Claim with new and relevant evidence has no deadline, though the effective date then generally starts no earlier than when VA receives it.

Not this form. You can't ask for two Board appeals in a row on the same claim. Before you start

The three dockets

Part II asks you to pick one. Each docket is its own line, and appeals are generally decided in the order they are docketed.

The three Board dockets compared
Docket (Part II)What the judge seesYour evidence windowVA's goal
10A Direct reviewWhat the judge sees: Only the evidence VA had when it decided. No hearing.Evidence window: NoneVA's goal: 365 days (1 year)
10B Evidence submissionWhat the judge sees: That record plus new evidence you send. No hearing.Evidence window: With the form, or within 90 days after the Board receives itVA's goal: 550 days (1.5 years)
10C HearingWhat the judge sees: That record plus your testimony and new evidence.Evidence window: At the hearing, or within 90 days after itVA's goal: 730 days (2 years)
  • The docket sets the record. On direct review, the Board generally won't consider evidence you send later. Evidence sent outside a 90-day window isn't part of the record for that appeal.
  • Hearing changes. If you withdraw your hearing request, you have 90 days from the Board's receipt of the withdrawal to send evidence. If you miss the hearing and it isn't rescheduled, you have 90 days from the scheduled date.
  • The goals are goals. VA.gov lists them as targets, not promised wait times.
  • Remands. If the judge finds VA missed a duty-to-assist step, such as a record or an adequate exam, and can't grant the benefit, the Board remands it to VA to fix the error and decide again.

The instructions on one page

What the 10182 instructions say

1. When to use it

  • For VA decisions dated on or after February 19, 2019.
  • Not for a Supplemental Claim (20-0995) or Higher-Level Review (20-0996).

2. Deadline

  • Generally within one year of the date VA mailed the decision.
  • After a legacy SOC or SSOC: 60 days from that letter or one year from the original decision, whichever is later.
  • Contested claim: 60 days.

3. Where to send it

  • Board of Veterans' Appeals, P.O. Box 27063, Washington, DC 20038.
  • Or online at VA.gov, or in person at a VA regional office.

4. Pick one docket

  • Check only one of 10A, 10B, or 10C.
  • Different dockets for different issues? Use a separate form for each.
  • Hearing: pick Central Office, videoconference, or virtual telehearing.

5. Issues

  • List each issue (11A) and the date of VA's decision (11B).
  • Extra sheets: put the veteran's last name and file number on each.
  • An unclear form: the Board contacts you to clarify.

6. Changing docket and signing

  • Change docket with a new 10182 within one year of the decision or 60 days of Board receipt, whichever is later, and before sending evidence or testimony.
  • Sign Item 12 in ink. An appointed representative may sign.

Before you send it

What the Board checks first

A problem with any of these can delay the appeal or leave an issue out of it.

1. It is signed and dated (Items 12 and 13).

Sign in ink: you, or your appointed representative. Part IV

2. Every issue is listed with its decision date (Item 11).

The Board may dismiss an appeal that doesn't identify the decision and the issues. The date shows the appeal is on time. Part III

3. Exactly one docket is checked (Item 10).

If the Board can't tell which docket you chose, it asks you to clarify, and the clock keeps running. Part II

Also check

  • It reaches the Board within one year of the decision letter (60 days for a contested claim).
  • Hearing request? One hearing type is checked, and for a virtual telehearing your email and representative are in Part I. Part II
  • Evidence docket? Your new evidence is attached or calendared to arrive within 90 days.
  • Your mailing address, phone, and email are current, so Board letters and hearing notices reach you. Part I

What it asks

The veteran's name, file number, and date of birth (Items 1–3). If you're not the veteran, such as a surviving spouse, your own name and date of birth (4–5). Then your mailing address, with a box if you're experiencing homelessness, phone, email, and your representative's name (6–9).

Why it matters

The Board writes to you here: docket letters, hearing notices, and its decision. A virtual telehearing needs the email address and representative in this part.

VA form 10182 Part I

IIBoard review optionItem 10: check only one

What it asks

  • 10A Direct review: no hearing, no new evidence. The form says it is often the fastest.
  • 10B Evidence submission: send evidence with the form or within 90 days after the Board receives it. The form notes this may lengthen the wait.
  • 10C Hearing: testify before a judge and send evidence within 90 days after. Pick one type: Central Office (in person in Washington, DC, at your own travel cost), videoconference (at a VA location), or virtual telehearing (from your own internet-connected device).

Why it matters

Your choice sets both the evidence the judge can consider and the line you wait in. Think about what you need: if VA misread the evidence it had, direct review fits. If the denial was for missing evidence, such as a medical opinion, you need a docket that lets you send it, or a Supplemental Claim.

Changing your mind

File a new 10182 with the new docket within one year of the decision or 60 days after the Board received the first one, whichever is later. You can't change once you've sent evidence or testimony for that appeal. A moved appeal keeps its original docket date.

Hearings

  • One hearing per appeal. It is informal, with no cross-examination, and is transcribed for your file.
  • Need a new date? Ask in writing with good cause at least two weeks before.
  • Missed it without a postponement? It is treated as withdrawn. You have 15 days to file a written motion showing good cause.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 20.202(b)–(c); 20.302; 20.303; 20.700; 20.704; 20.800(a)(2) VA form 10182 Part II

IIISpecific issues to be appealedItem 11: the heart of the form

What it asks

Each specific issue you disagree with (11A) and the date of VA's decision on it (11B). Column C and attached sheets hold additional issues; put the veteran's last name and file number on each sheet.

Why it matters

Only the issues you list are appealed. Copy each issue as the decision letter states it, such as "Evaluation of left knee condition" or "Service connection for tinnitus." The Board reads the form liberally, but it may dismiss an appeal that doesn't identify the decision and issues.

The three boxes

  • Decision as soon as possible: asks the Board to decide even while you could still change dockets. You give up the chance to change once it decides. The form says it does not change your place in line.
  • Extension of time: for a late appeal, check this and attach your good-cause explanation. The Board decides whether good cause exists.
  • Additional sheets attached.

If the Board can't tell

If it can't tell which issues or which docket you chose, the Board contacts you. Answer within 60 days of its letter or one year of the decision, whichever is later, or those issues are not appealed.

After you file

What happens next

  • A docket letter. If the Board accepts the appeal, it writes to confirm it is on the docket. Unless the Board asks for something, you don't need to do anything while you wait. Check status at VA.gov.
  • Your effective date is protected. A Board appeal filed within one year keeps the issue continuously pursued. So does a Supplemental Claim filed within one year after the Board decides.
  • One lane at a time. While the appeal is pending, you can't file a Supplemental Claim or Higher-Level Review on the same issue.
  • Moving up the line. If you're 75 or older, seriously ill, or in severe financial hardship, you or your representative can ask in writing to advance the appeal. Give the reason, the veteran's name, and the VA file number. The Board doesn't advance a case unless someone asks.
  • Withdrawing. You can withdraw in writing before the Board decides, and still file another review if it would be on time.
  • After the decision. Appeal to the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims within 120 days, or file a Supplemental Claim with new and relevant evidence.

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Choosing direct review when the denial was for missing evidence.
The judge sees only what VA had. If you need a new medical opinion or records in the file, pick evidence submission or a hearing, or consider a Supplemental Claim.
Picking the evidence docket, then sending evidence late.
Evidence that arrives after the 90-day window isn't part of the record for that appeal. Send it with the form if it's ready.
Writing a letter instead of using the form.
The Board accepts only VA Form 10182. A letter or a different VA form isn't a Notice of Disagreement and doesn't stop the one-year clock.
Leaving out an issue or its decision date.
Only listed issues are appealed, and a missing date makes timeliness hard to confirm.
Trying to switch dockets after sending evidence.
Once you've sent evidence or testimony for the appeal, the docket can't change.
Filing a second Board appeal after the Board decides.
The next steps are the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (120 days) or a Supplemental Claim.
Not asking to advance the appeal when you qualify.
Age 75 or older, serious illness, or severe financial hardship can move you up, but only if you ask in writing.
