VA Form 20-0995, Supplemental Claim, section by section

VA Form 20-0995 asks VA to look at a decided issue again because you have new and relevant evidence. This guide explains what each section asks for and why it matters under public law. It explains the form; it does not fill it out, and nothing you do here is saved or sent.

Is this the right form for you?

Pick the one that fits. The answers come from the form's "When to Use This Form" instructions. Nothing you pick is saved.

Use VA Form 20-0995. You can send the new evidence or tell VA where to get it. File within one year of the decision notice to keep your earliest effective date. See Timing and your date.

Consider VA Form 20-0996, Higher-Level Review. A more senior reviewer looks at the same evidence. You can't add new evidence in that lane. If you also have new evidence, a Supplemental Claim may fit better.

Use VA Form 10182 for a Board appeal. The form lists it as the third option. See ClaimReady's VA Form 10182 guide.

Not this form. The form says that if your condition got worse, use VA Form 21-526EZ to ask for an increased evaluation.

Use VA Form 20-0995. You can file a Supplemental Claim at any time, but if it arrives more than a year after the decision, the earliest date generally becomes the date VA receives it. Also check Section VI: you need to acknowledge the evidence notice.

Before you start

Timing and your date

Within one year

If you file within one year of the decision notice, and keep filing the next review within a year of each decision, VA treats the issue as continuously pursued. A grant can then go back to your original claim date, or when entitlement arose if later.

ClaimReady: Continuous Pursuit Review

After one year

You can still file, but the effective date is generally no earlier than the date VA receives the Supplemental Claim.

  • One lane at a time. While one review of an issue is pending, you can't file a different review of that same issue.
  • Each issue has its own year. Issues you don't list on this form still have one year from the decision notice for their own Supplemental Claim.
  • One benefit type per form. Use a separate form for each benefit type, such as compensation and pension.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b), (c), (h)(1)–(2) VA form 20-0995 instructions, Item 1 and Section IV

The instructions on one page

What the 20-0995 instructions say

1. When to use it

  • You disagree with a VA decision and have new and relevant evidence.
  • Worse condition: use 21-526EZ. Other choices: 20-0996 Higher-Level Review, 10182 Board appeal.

2. Where to send it

  • To the intake center for your benefit type. VA recommends filing electronically.
  • One benefit type per form.

3. Help

  • An accredited representative, or VA at 1-800-827-1000.
  • You can ask for a hearing at any time.
  • Attorneys and agents generally may charge only after VA's initial decision.

4. Issues (Section IV)

  • List each issue and the date of the decision notice.
  • Only listed issues are reviewed. Unlisted issues keep their own one-year window.
  • Within a year of a court decision: list that date and attach a copy.

5. Evidence (Section V)

  • Send new and relevant evidence, or identify records for VA to get.
  • VA medical center, VA clinic, and military treatment records need no consent form.
  • Private and Vet Center records: 21-4142 and 21-4142a.
  • A change in law needs no new evidence.

6. Notice and signature

  • Sign Item 25A. Witnesses only if you sign with an "X."
  • Check Yes in Item 23 (5103 notice) after reviewing VA's evidence notice. Required more than a year after the decision for compensation, pension, DIC, and accrued claims. "No" means VA mails the notice first, which causes delay.

Before you send it

What a reviewer checks first

The first two matter most. A problem with either one stops or delays the review.

1. It is signed and dated (Item 25).

An unsigned form is not a complete claim. Section VIII

2. The 5103 notice box in Section VI is checked "Yes" (Item 23).

Checking it confirms you reviewed VA's notice of the evidence your claim needs, so VA does not have to send that notice first. Leaving it blank, or checking "No," means VA mails the notice before it moves forward, which delays the review. The form requires it for compensation, pension, DIC, and accrued claims filed more than a year after the decision. Section VI

Also check

  • One benefit type is checked in Item 1. Item 1
  • Each issue is listed with its decision date in Item 21, copied from the decision letter. Section IV
  • New and relevant evidence is sent or identified. Section V
  • VA treatment is listed; private treatment has a 21-4142. Item 22

1Benefit typeItem 1: check only one

What it asks

Which benefit the decision was about: compensation; pension, DIC, or survivors benefits; fiduciary; education; loan guaranty; life insurance; Veteran Readiness and Employment; National Cemetery Administration; or Veterans Health Administration.

Why it matters

Each benefit type goes to a different intake center. Check only one box, and use a separate form for another benefit type.

I–IIVeteran and claimant informationItems 2–19

What it asks

Section I is the veteran's information. Section II is only for a claimant who is not the veteran, such as a surviving spouse, child, parent, or fiduciary.

Why it matters

The instructions say that a last name with a Social Security number or VA file number lets VA identify you even if other fields are blank. Keep your mailing address current: VA may schedule an exam or ask for records by mail.

VA form 20-0995 instructions, Sections I and II

IIIHomeless informationItem 20: only if you are homeless or at risk

Why it matters

It gives VA a point of contact who can reach you. The options include living somewhere not meant for sleeping, a shelter, staying with friends or family, or losing your home or a facility placement in the next 30 days. Skip to Section IV if none applies.

VA form 20-0995 Item 20

IVIssues for reviewItem 21: the heart of the form

What it asks

Each issue VA decided that you want reviewed (21A), and the date of the VA decision notice for it (21B).

Why it matters

VA reviews only the issues you list. Copy each issue the way the decision letter states it, such as "Service connection for sleep apnea" or "Evaluation of PTSD," so VA can match it. The decision date tells VA whether you filed within a year.

Watch for

  • An issue left off still has its own year from the decision notice, but filing it later means another form.
  • Within a year of a decision from the Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims or a higher court, list the court decision date and attach a copy.
  • For an older (legacy) appeal, the instructions describe a 60-day window after a Statement of the Case to opt in.

Law 38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(h) VA form 20-0995 instructions, Section IV

VNew and relevant evidenceItem 22: where VA should look

What counts

New means it was not part of the record VA had before. Relevant means it tends to prove or disprove something at issue, including a new theory of how the condition is connected. If you don't send or identify any, VA issues a decision saying there was not enough evidence to readjudicate.

What Item 22 asks

Where you were treated: private providers, Vet Centers, VA-paid community care, VA medical centers and clinics, military treatment facilities, or other. Give the name and location and approximate dates, or check the box if you don't have dates.

VA's duty to assist

  • A substantially complete Supplemental Claim triggers VA's duty to help get the evidence.
  • VA can get VA medical center, VA clinic, and military treatment records without a consent form.
  • For private and Vet Center records, sign VA Forms 21-4142 and 21-4142a, or send the records yourself.
  • If a record holder refuses or charges a fee, VA will tell you.

For how new evidence can lead to a new exam, read ClaimReady's Supplemental Claims guide.

Law 38 C.F.R. §§ 3.2501, 3.159(c) VA form 20-0995 instructions, Section V and Item 22

VIEvidence notice acknowledgmentItem 23: check "Yes" to avoid delay

What it asks

For compensation, pension, DIC, and accrued benefits filed more than one year after the decision notice: review VA's online notice of the evidence needed, then check Yes in Item 23. If you can't review it online, check No and VA will mail it to you.

Why it matters

VA must tell you what evidence is needed to support a claim. This box is how you confirm you got that notice. If it is left blank or answered "No," VA mails the notice first, which delays the review.

Law 38 U.S.C. § 5103 VA form 20-0995 Section VI

VIIHealth care notification optionItem 24: claims tied to a personal traumatic event

What it asks

If your claim is for a condition from a personal traumatic event, such as military sexual trauma, and you are enrolled in VA health care, you can let VA's benefits side tell VA health care about upcoming exams, Board hearings, and and decision notices. You can consent, decline, revoke an earlier consent, or mark it not applicable.

Why it matters

An answer isn't required, and the form says your choice does not affect the outcome of the claim.

VA form 20-0995 Section VII

VIII–XISignaturesItems 25–29: you, witnesses, alternate signer, representative

Section VIII: your signature

You certify the statements are true and correct to the best of your knowledge and belief. An unsigned form isn't a complete claim.

Sections IX–XI

  • Witnesses: only if you sign with an "X."
  • Alternate signer: only if Item 25A is blank, with VA Form 21-0972 on file.
  • Representative: needs VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a on file, and asks for the accreditation number and when that form was submitted.

Common mistakes

What often goes wrong

Not signing, or skipping the 5103 notice box.
These are the two most common causes of delay. Sign Item 25 and check "Yes" in Item 23.
Sending the same evidence again.
Evidence already in the record isn't new. Point to something VA didn't have, or a theory it didn't address.
Using this form for a condition that got worse.
A worsening is a claim for increase on the 21-526EZ.
Leaving off the decision date.
VA uses it to match the issue and to see whether you're within the year.
Filing a Higher-Level Review and a Supplemental Claim on the same issue at once.
Only one review of an issue can be pending at a time.
Missing the one-year window.
Filing within a year keeps the earliest effective date; after that, the date is generally when VA receives the form.
Not signing a 21-4142 for private records.
VA needs your consent to request them, or you can send them yourself.
